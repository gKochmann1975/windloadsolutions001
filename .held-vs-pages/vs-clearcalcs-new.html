  • You need tornado-load output in the same ASCE 7-22 template as your wind.

WindLoadCalc vs ClearCalcs — questions

Is ClearCalcs accurate enough for ASCE 7-22 wind?

Yes. ClearCalcs computes ASCE 7-22 C&C and MWFRS wind, exposes GCp/GCpi with equation references, and runs a traffic-light check system. On coefficient transparency the two products are genuinely even — we call that a tie. The divergence shows up after the math: ClearCalcs stops at the raw ASCE map wind speed, while WindLoadCalc layers the FBC HVHZ override on top and returns the value a Florida reviewer will actually enforce.

Does ClearCalcs apply the Florida Building Code HVHZ wind speed automatically?

Not in any way their published wind page documents. On Calcs.com the basic wind speed comes from the ASCE 7-22 Figure 26.5-1 maps or manual entry, with no Miami-Dade, Broward, or Collier override layer described. WindLoadCalc auto-applies FBC R301.2(7) so a Miami-Dade project returns the enforceable 175 mph instead of only the interpolated map reading.

Can I run a wind number on ClearCalcs for free?

Only temporarily. ClearCalcs gives a 14-day Pro-level trial that downgrades to read-only afterward, and wind is not included in the $66 Basic tier — you move to Pro (listed at $99/mo annual or $119/mo monthly) to keep creating calcs. WindLoadCalc keeps a public wind-speed-by-ZIP plus single-component pressure tool open with no account and no card, under a soft daily cap.

ClearCalcs covers wood, steel, concrete and footings — why use a wind-only tool?

If your day is mixed member design, that breadth is the right reason to keep ClearCalcs — it is a real strength and we don't pretend otherwise. But a broad suite still leaves two things undone for a permit: it hands you the ASCE map value rather than the enforceable Florida number, and it cannot stamp anything. WindLoadCalc fills exactly those gaps for the wind portion of the job.

Does ClearCalcs offer a PE sign-and-seal service?

No. ClearCalcs is design-calculation software; nothing on its wind or pricing pages offers a sealed deliverable in any jurisdiction, so you still have to locate and pay a separate engineer to stamp the output. WindLoadCalc routes the finished calc through an in-house Florida P.E. and a partner-PE network that signs and seals in all 50 states — the calculation and the seal live in one workflow.

What about tornado loads, which ClearCalcs supports?

That's a fair point in their favor: ClearCalcs produces separate ASCE 7-22 tornado pressures when a tornado speed is supplied, and WindLoadCalc does not document tornado output today. If tornado design inside the same template is a hard requirement, weigh that honestly. For straight hurricane-region wind and a sealed permit package, the specialist still closes the loop the suite leaves open.
