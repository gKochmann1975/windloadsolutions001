Who should genuinely pick ENERCALC

A practicing structural or civil PE, or a small-to-mid firm, that needs one everyday suite to design steel, wood, concrete, masonry, and foundation members — not just run wind loads — and is happy signing its own sheets. The 64 modules, ASD/LRFD coverage, legacy-edition support, and optional 3D FEM make it the stronger daily driver when wind is a thin slice of the workload.

If you never need a stamped deliverable, an open public calc, a CAD-ready schedule, or automatic Florida HVHZ values, ENERCALC's breadth is a legitimate, well-established choice. We'll say so plainly.

But when wind is what's slowing the permit — the lookup, the HVHZ value, the schedule, the seal — those are exactly the steps ENERCALC leaves on your plate and the ones WindLoadCalc carries to the finish.

ENERCALC comparison FAQ

If ENERCALC already handles my whole building, why add a wind-only tool?

Because the two solve different jobs and ENERCALC stops right where permits get issued. ENERCALC is excellent at member design across steel, wood, concrete, masonry, and foundations, but for wind it sends you to the external ASCE Hazard Tool for the basic speed and produces no stamped deliverable. WindLoadCalc finishes that slice — built-in ZIP wind speeds, automatic Florida HVHZ enforcement, AutoCAD schedules, and a licensed-PE seal — so you can keep ENERCALC for members and still hand a client a sealed wind package.

Where does ENERCALC get its basic wind speed?

On its own ASCE 7-22 wind module pages, ENERCALC links out to the external ASCE Hazard Tool at ascehazardtool.org for the basic wind speed — you leave the calc, fetch the number, and bring it back. WindLoadCalc keeps the speed in-app: enter a project ZIP and it returns pre-verified values for all four Risk Categories from 33,783 US ZIP codes in roughly half a minute, no second website required.

Does ENERCALC stamp or seal the wind calculation for me?

No. ENERCALC is a software subscription; its product line and pricing include no sign-and-seal service in any state, so the engineer of record signs their own output. WindLoadCalc pairs the wind calculation with an actual sealing service — an in-house Florida P.E. plus a partner-PE network that signs and seals in all 50 states — so the numbers and the stamp come from one place.

Can I test ENERCALC's wind module without paying?

Only on the clock. ENERCALC provides a 30-day free trial, after which any use requires a paid subscription; there is no permanently-free public wind tool in its lineup. WindLoadCalc keeps an open public calculator — wind speed by ZIP plus a single-component design pressure — that needs no account and no card, under a soft daily cap, so you can sanity-check a real address any time.

Does ENERCALC apply the Florida HVHZ value automatically?

ENERCALC can run an ASCE 7-22 wind calc for a Florida project, but its documentation describes no automatic Miami-Dade or HVHZ override — the basic speed still comes from the external Hazard Tool and is entered by hand. WindLoadCalc auto-applies the FBC 8th Edition (2023) HVHZ rule, so a Miami-Dade address returns the enforceable 175 mph under FBC R301.2(7) rather than only the interpolated map reading.

Will ENERCALC give me an AutoCAD-ready opening schedule?

ENERCALC's documented deliverables are structural calculation reports, not a drawing-ready spreadsheet for a drafter. WindLoadCalc exports a genuine .xlsx Architectural Schedule made to drop straight into an AutoCAD opening set, on top of the usual PDF, Excel, CSV, and print, and it tracks FL# Product Approval and Miami-Dade NOA certifications for those openings too.
