Straight answers

Excel wind load calculator — your questions.

Is WindLoadCalc a wind load calculator in Excel?

No. WindLoadCalc is browser-based ASCE 7-22 software, not a spreadsheet. It gives you what people wanted from an Excel wind load calculator without the broken formulas.

And it exports to Excel — plus PDF, HTML and CSV — whenever you want a spreadsheet copy.

Can I export my wind load results to Excel?

Yes. Excel (.xlsx) is one of several one-click exports, alongside PDF, HTML, CSV, and a plan-ready schedule format.

The calculation lives in the software, so Excel is simply an output you can generate any time — not the fragile place your engineering lives.

Why don't you offer ASCE 7-16 or an older edition?

On purpose. Building codes are rolling over to ASCE 7-22, so we build the current standard and add new editions as they become relevant instead of maintaining decade-old math.

Even where a jurisdiction still cites an older edition, designing to a wind standard more than ten years out of date does not serve the people under the roof.

Can my team and subcontractors share access?

Yes. Add or remove a teammate or subcontractor by email in your Account access panel.

Your company and its subs work the same live projects — nothing gets emailed around.

Do I have to re-enter my building for each calculator?

No. A saved project carries across calculators — run components and cladding for the windows, doors and shutters, then open MWFRS or the roof C&C and the building is already filled in.

Then just print or export.

Is the exported report a sealed or stamped document?

No — what you export is an Engineering Report, never a sealed or stamped document. Sign-and-seal by a professional engineer is a separate service, available in all 50 states through the firm's engineer network, that you add only when a project specifically calls for a stamp.
