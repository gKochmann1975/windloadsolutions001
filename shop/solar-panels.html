Why not one calculator for both?

Because a panel on a roof and a panel in a field don't share a flow field, an exposure, or a failure point. Forcing one set of coefficients onto both is how solar arrays get under-designed.

The flow field

RooftopSits in the building's separated flow; GCrn coefficients.
GroundOpen inclined surface with air on both sides; net front/rear pressure.

The exposure

RooftopTakes the building's height and surroundings; parapet can shelter.
GroundFlat open land, usually Exposure C or D — nothing bleeds the speed off.

Where it fails

RooftopClip pull-out or ballast sliding on the membrane.
GroundPile uplift and overturning at the post foundation.

Common questions

Which one do I need?

If the panels attach to a building's roof, use the rooftop calculator. If they stand on their own racking in a yard or field, use the ground-mount calculator. Not sure? The rooftop tool is for anything mounted on a roof surface; everything free-standing on the ground is the ground-mount tool.

Is it really the same ASCE standard?

Yes — both calculators work from ASCE 7-22. They differ in which provisions apply: rooftop arrays use the Section 29.4 nominal net pressure coefficients, while free-standing arrays are treated as open, elevated inclined surfaces with pressure on both faces. Same code, two correct paths.

Will there be one subscription or two?

Each calculator is its own tool, and you'll be able to add just the one you need — or bundle both with the other calculators. Pricing goes live with the products; join a notify list on either page and you'll get early access at launch-day rates.

Can a PE seal the output?

Yes. Whichever calculator you use, a licensed professional engineer can review and seal the result through the firm's engineer network, routed to a PE registered in the project's state. Sealing is a separate professional service added when the jurisdiction requires it.
